## Interpretation (392 words)
The pair named here is row (d) in 2.1, which is also the pair plotted in Figure 7.

Across all 1,461 days in the supplied cleaned file (19 June 2014 to 31 December 2018), North Brisbane cyclists and Bicentennial pedestrians show almost no linear relationship (r = −0.028, S2 Box 5). The negative sign is too small to describe as an inverse relationship.

Figure 7 shows that this near-zero value hides two separate groups. Before June 2015, most Bicentennial pedestrian counts sit between about 1,000 and 2,500 a day and rise with North Brisbane cycling (r = 0.469). From June 2015 they sit almost entirely below 1,000, with a weaker positive relationship (r = 0.216) (S2 Box 7). The correlation is also positive within every calendar year, from 0.109 in 2017 to 0.382 in 2015 (S2 Box 8).

The pooled coefficient is weak because the two series moved in opposite directions between these periods. Average Bicentennial pedestrians fell from 1,440 to 648 a day, while North Brisbane cyclists rose from 91 to 144 (S2 Box 7). Combining the two groups cancels the positive day-to-day association, which is the subgroup problem covered in Week 8. The pedestrian drop matches the mid-2015 change at the Bicentennial counter identified in Section 1, when recorded pedestrians fell and recorded cyclists rose by a similar amount, so it probably reflects how users were classified rather than fewer people walking. The same shift explains the negative coefficient in row (a) (r = −0.355).

Counter faults kept in the supplied file weaken the coefficient further. The line of points at zero pedestrians and the line at zero cyclists in Figure 7 are the 72 and 40 zero days identified as recording problems in Section 1 (S2 Box 9).

The positive relationship within each period most likely reflects influences that affect both paths on the same days, such as weekday commuting, holidays and weather, rather than cycling at Windsor changing walking on the Bicentennial Bikeway. Correlation does not show causation.

For BCC, the pooled coefficient should not be read as meaning the two paths are unrelated. Once the counter change is accounted for, daily use at the two sites rises and falls together to a weak-to-moderate degree. Neither count predicts the other well enough to replace direct counting, so each upgrade should be planned from that path's own corrected counts.
