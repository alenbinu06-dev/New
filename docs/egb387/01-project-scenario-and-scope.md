# 1. Project scenario and scope

## 1.1 Preamble (why the project exists)

- For several years homeowners have been encouraged to install rooftop solar on residential
  properties, via substantial government payments for electricity generated beyond household
  needs, plus subsidy of the installation cost of the panels.
- Consequently owners installed panel numbers well beyond that required to provide hot water —
  hot water being the only practical form of storing that energy.
- Battery storage of the surplus for night-time use, and for periods when solar generation is
  seriously degraded, has been prevented as an option by the **high cost and physical size** of
  the batteries required.
- Instead the surplus is fed back into the mains, and any shortfall in household generation at
  other times is made up by drawing on normal mains supply.
- The result: current flows into the mains at times of strong sunlight, causing serious problems
  for the supply authorities, and there is heavy demand on supply at times of low solar activity.
  The effect is **random** and does little to reduce the level of normal generation.

## 1.2 The project

- A project has been developed to use the surplus to **pump water to an elevated dam** for later
  release through **hydro-electric generators**, returning energy to the system.
- The flexibility of this system permits **matching of supply to times of peak demand**.
- Proposed location: **Enoggera Reservoir**. The site advantage cited is **close proximity to a
  large source of solar energy supply**.

## 1.3 The six works elements

The proposal requires construction of several civil engineering structures together with
ancillary mechanical and electrical systems:

1. A dam in the foothills **immediately westwards of the Reservoir**.
2. A small **Power Station**.
3. A **Pumping Station**.
4. A **steel penstock** from the dam to the combined Pump and Power Station.
5. A **transmission line connection** to the suburban electrical system adjacent to the site.
6. **Ancillary tasks**, including **dredging in the ponded area** of the Reservoir, and **road works**.

## 1.4 Your role (scope of the assignment)

- You are a **recent graduate** working for a **medium sized construction company** which is
  **tendering** for construction of the facility.
- Your task is to **assist the company estimators by developing the construction procedures** to
  be used for the **three major areas of work**: the dam, the power/pump station, and the major
  pipeline.
- Once methods are decided you are to:
  - **select the equipment needed**, and
  - **identify such temporary works as are required** to implement those methods, which requires
    you to undertake the **conceptual design of those temporary works**.

## 1.5 The access bridge (Design and Construct component)

- The Client has decided that the **small access bridge** to the Pump and Power Station **depends
  on the construction methods adopted** and on the **degree of site assembly** envisaged by the
  Electrical and Mechanical Contractors, since this will affect the **loading**.
- For that reason the bridge is to be bid as a **Design and Construct component** of the overall
  tender.
- Your role in the bridge D&C component is to **investigate alternatives and select suitable
  construction methods for your chosen design**.
- You are **not required to undertake a full design of the bridge** — that will be done by others
  in the company.

## 1.6 Programming, supervision and stakeholders

- You must develop a **preliminary construction program** ensuring completion in the allocated time.
- From that program you must assess:
  - the **supervision demands** of the work,
  - the **working hours**, and
  - the **time that major plant will be on site**.
- You must also **deal with stakeholders**, in particular **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander**
  peoples, and the impact of the project on them.

## 1.7 Coordination obligation

- **Three companies have been nominated by the Client** to provide the **electrical, mechanical
  and dredging** functions.
- **Coordination of these activities is part of your brief as the Principal Contractor.**

## 1.8 Explicitly out of scope — costing

- You are **not required to cost your methods** in this unit; it is intended this will be done in a
  later Estimating unit.
- Because you do not have recourse to costing as a final determinant of your choices, **decisions
  are judged on the appropriateness of the methods chosen to cope with the physical conditions
  inherent in the work**.
- Guidance on this is given during lectures and tutorials.

## 1.9 Degree of effort (stated expectation)

- There is a **high level of integration of the lectures and tutorials with the project**.
- The teaching is **heavily experience based**; **sporadic attendance at either lectures or
  tutorials will result in an inadequate understanding**.
- You should work on the project over a period of **7 weeks**, producing a project executive plan.
- It is **expected you will report progress on a weekly basis** as part of the submission; your
  contribution is **assessed via your engagement with the group**.

## 1.10 Resources and support available

Four weeks of lectures, the project briefing document, project data, and tutorial exercises.
