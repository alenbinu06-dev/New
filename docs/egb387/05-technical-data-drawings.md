### Face profiles (important for formwork)

- The **downstream (left) face is stepped** — the classic stepped RCC profile, formed in lifts.
- The **upstream (right) face is near vertical**.
- On the **Section at Spillway** the downstream face is instead a **smooth chute** (no steps),
  i.e. the spillway is a formed and finished overflow surface, not stepped RCC.

This step/chute distinction is the single most formwork-relevant feature on the drawings, and
criterion 3 of the group CRA explicitly requires **formwork** to be covered.

## 5.6 Sub-strata section

The legend lists four strata; the section shows them in this order from the **surface downwards**:

1. **Alluvial silt and gravel** (thin surface layer)
2. **Clay and gravel**
3. **Weathered granite (some clay seams)**
4. **Unweathered granite (some fracturing)**

The section shows the Pump Station structure founded down through the upper layers, and the
**vehicle bridge piles** driven down through the strata alongside it, with **average water level
RL 75.0** marked. Ground rises steeply to the east (the bank side).

Construction implications flagged by these descriptions:

- **Unweathered granite with some fracturing** at depth explains the **grout curtain** beneath the
  dam cut-off wall — and the fact that **curtain grouting is one of only two permitted subcontract
  packages**.
- **Weathered granite** is consistent with ripping using the owned **D8 dozers equipped with
  rippers**; **unweathered granite** implies **drill and blast** or rock breaking (owned air-operated
  paving breakers and 600 cfm compressors). Noise curfew and adjacent suburbs constrain blasting.
- **Alluvial silt and gravel over clay and gravel over rock** at the Pump Station is the
  driveability condition for the **steel tube piles** and any **sheet pile cofferdam**, and the
  reason a **dredged channel** is required.
- Granite excavated on site is the obvious candidate **aggregate source for the RCC dam** — the
  briefing explicitly requires the dam method to cover "**including obtaining the materials**", and
  this is also **individual reflective report topic 2**.

## 5.7 Derived figures (calculated, not stated on the drawings)

- **Freeboard**: RL 102 − RL 100 = **2 m**.
- **Maximum dam height**: RL 102 − RL 86 = **16 m** at the deepest point; the founding level
  follows contours, so height varies along the 250 m crest.
- **Base width to height ratio**: 13 m base ÷ 16 m height ≈ **0.81**, consistent with a gravity RCC
  section.
- **Gross head check**: RL 100 top water level − RL 70 inlet invert = **30 m**, reconciling with the
  "Max. Head 30 m" annotation.
- **Head to the Pump Station water surface**: RL 100 − RL 75.0 = **25 m**.
- **Pump Station structure height**: RL 83.0 − RL 67.0 = **16 m** overall, of which **RL 75.0 −
  RL 67.0 = 8 m is below average water level**.
- **Dredging depth at the intake**: existing bed down to the **RL 68.5** dredged channel level; the
  channel invert sits **1.5 m below the screen invert (RL 70.0)** and **6.5 m below average water
  level**.
- The **8 m depth below water** is the governing figure for the cofferdam / dewatering / underwater
  works method — the subject of **individual reflective report topic 3**.
- **Sheet pile length check**: 1900 m of salvaged sheet piling is available in "random lengths"; an
  8 m-deep excavation below water needs piles materially longer than 8 m for toe penetration and
  freeboard, so the random-lengths note is a real constraint to address.

## 5.8 Ambiguity to flag (not resolved by the drawings)

The elevation gives a **lowest operating water level of RL 95**, i.e. a **5 m** drawdown from
RL 100. The cross-sections annotate a **"normal operating range 2 m"** below RL 100, i.e. down to
RL 98. The two do not reconcile. The most likely reading is that **2 m is the normal day-to-day
cycling range** and **RL 95 is the extreme lower operating limit**, but this should be confirmed
before any storage-volume or program calculation relies on it.
